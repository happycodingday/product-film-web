# Optional process and experience shots

Choose techniques that make this product easier to understand. These are creative options, not a fixed story structure.

## Explain a mechanism

Start with a readable overview if the process needs one. Highlight the current step, animate its action, and return to the overview before moving on. Show relevant inputs becoming outcomes rather than relying on abstract arrows or labels. Keep diagrams simple enough to read during playback.

## Show an everyday experience

Place the product in a relevant situation. Animate a concrete need, a user action, and its result. A selection of miniature situations can introduce different uses; let one take focus when it becomes the main scene. Use related objects and gestures to make the experience recognizable, with enough space around the subject.

## Reveal a transformation

Use successive states, an exploded view, a material change, a comparison, or a gradual assembly when it explains the product. Preserve continuity between states and keep the result visible. Labels and intermediate layers must not cover the main subject.

## Compare choices or outcomes

Use consistent framing and scale for before/after or side-by-side comparisons. Highlight one meaningful difference at a time. Make parameter changes visible in the result when interaction is relevant. Base measurements and performance claims on actual evidence.

## Show a review or feedback loop

If review is relevant to the product, show a concrete issue, a deliberate change, and a checked result. Checkmarks are illustrations of the process, not proof that a real test ran. Distinguish a simulated workflow from observed product behavior.

All animation and optional click-to-seek controls share the master clock. Leave time for viewers to understand each result.
